Master of Architecture Thesis · 2024

Mayfield Depot

Circular adaptive reuse of Manchester’s former railway depot as a connected ecological, cultural and energy-producing urban infrastructure.

Location

Manchester, United Kingdom

Year

2023–2024

Type

Master of Architecture thesis

Authors

Devesh Mishra
Harshvardhan S. Chouhan

Supervisors

Prof. Paola Altamura
Prof. Laura Pedata

01 / Overview

From infrastructural barrier to regenerative urban commons

The project reimagines Mayfield Depot and its elevated railway as a multi-layered system connecting heritage, ecology, public life and circular resource flows.

Located beside Manchester Piccadilly Station, Mayfield Park and the River Medlock, the former depot occupies a strategic but fragmented part of the city.

The proposal operates across neighbourhood, site, building and environmental-system scales. Existing structures are retained while new public routes, cultural programmes, productive landscapes and flood-responsive spaces reconnect the depot with its surroundings.

How can adaptive reuse transform dismissed railway heritage into an eco-efficient urban infrastructure without erasing its architectural identity?

02 / Analysis

Reading the city before drawing the intervention

Anthropic, biophysical and bioclimatic analyses identified the site’s barriers, risks and regenerative capacity.

The research examined transport, green-blue systems, flood exposure, ecological opportunities and solar access.

These analytical layers were synthesised into a design brief rather than treated as isolated mapping exercises.

03 / Framework

Revitalize. Renurture. Reconnect.

Revitalize

Activate the depot, arches and infrastructural leftovers through cultural production, markets, learning, work and inclusive public programmes.

Renurture

Restore ecological processes through floodable landscapes, riparian planting, productive roofs, biodiversity and circular water systems.

Reconnect

Repair fragmentation through accessible pedestrian, cycling and ecological connections across multiple levels.

04 / Urban design

A connected landscape of public life, water and slow mobility

05 / Building

Adaptive reuse above, beneath and through the arches

The depot is retained as an industrial framework into which reversible, lightweight and climate-responsive elements are inserted.

The strategy creates a continuous public corridor, a productive roofscape, low-E glazed façades, box-in-box rooms and timber mezzanines.

New interventions remain visually distinct from the historic brick arches and steel structure.
